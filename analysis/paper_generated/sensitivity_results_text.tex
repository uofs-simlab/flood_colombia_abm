All 960 additional runs completed the same 408-hour horizon and passed the hourly, household, institutional and rescue-accounting checks. Four prespecified reference repetitions reproduced the primary-study numerical outputs exactly. Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity} and Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity-summary} show how the four mortality comparisons change when individual assumptions are varied. Each effect is the second setting minus the first, in percentage points; negative values mean lower mortality in the second setting. Brackets give 95\% Monte Carlo intervals unless identified as ranges across settings.

For the comparison from no organization to full, the primary mean mortality difference is -7.85 [-8.31, -7.40] percentage points. Across the 10 applicable sensitivity settings, mean differences range from -22.34 to -4.32. The largest change from the primary difference occurs in the ``Hospital: 1\%'' setting. The mean shift is -14.49 [-15.64, -13.33] percentage points.

For the comparison from no organization to social only, the primary mean mortality difference is +0.03 [-0.20, +0.26] percentage points. Across the 8 applicable sensitivity settings, mean differences range from -0.24 to +0.57. The largest change from the primary difference occurs in the ``Hospital: 1\%'' setting. The mean shift is +0.54 [-0.41, +1.49] percentage points.

For the comparison from near to far, 30\% capacity, the primary mean mortality difference is +1.10 [+0.81, +1.39] percentage points. Across the 6 applicable sensitivity settings, mean differences range from +0.62 to +6.85. The largest change from the primary difference occurs in the ``Hospital: 1\%'' setting. The mean shift is +5.75 [+4.50, +6.99] percentage points.

For the comparison from one to five boats, far, the primary mean mortality difference is -0.24 [-0.46, -0.03] percentage points. Across the 6 applicable sensitivity settings, mean differences range from -1.94 to -0.03. The largest change from the primary difference occurs in the ``Hospital: 1\%'' setting. The mean shift is -1.70 [-2.89, -0.51] percentage points.

The mortality levels in each scenario help explain the hospital-capacity results. Without organization, mean mortality is 22.53\%, 8.05\% and 7.48\% at hospital capacities of 1\%, 5\% and 10\%, respectively. Under full organization, the corresponding means are 0.19\%, 0.19\% and 0.19\%.

At 30\% shelter capacity and 5\% hospital capacity, mean mortality is 3.98\% at the near site and 5.08\% at the far site. With 1\% hospital capacity, these means are 7.88\% and 14.73\%, respectively. Hospital capacity therefore affects both mortality levels as well as the difference between locations.

After Holm adjustment across all 30 tests, 9 have $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}$ values below 0.05 (Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivity}). These tests ask whether a parameter changes a mortality contrast; they do not test whether the contrast itself is zero. Tested-effect ranges summarize the selected settings.
