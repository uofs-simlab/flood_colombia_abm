The 480-run primary experiment separates three mechanisms often aggregated under flood-response policy: exposure reduction, behavioural response and access to safe shelter and care. Running the organization channels separately distinguishes the assumed reduction in flood depth from changes to social-capital scores. The weaker-depth-reduction scenario shows how strongly the response depends on that physical assumption. The rescue experiments compare shelter locations and fleet sizes at specified shelter capacities. Their hourly records show why the number rescued is not enough to assess response: admission timing, length of stay and stranding duration also matter. These are findings about the study model, not proven real-world intervention effects. Independent hazard and response data, together with tests of alternative model structures, are needed before the results can guide operational decisions.
