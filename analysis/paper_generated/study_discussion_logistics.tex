At 30\% shelter capacity, moving from the near to the far site changes mean mortality by +1.10 percentage points (95\% Monte Carlo interval +0.81 to +1.39). At the far site, increasing the fleet from one to five boats at 10\% shelter capacity changes mean mortality by -0.24 percentage points (95\% Monte Carlo interval -0.46 to -0.03). This fleet comparison does not meet the 0.05 threshold after Holm adjustment within its family ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.0925$), although its unadjusted Monte Carlo interval excludes zero. The test adjusts for multiple comparisons, whereas the interval describes this comparison alone; they therefore need not give the same conclusion. The overall tests compare sites within each shelter capacity and fleets within each site. Comparing their adjusted probabilities does not test whether one resource changes the effect of another. The results depend on the assumed routes, facility protection, injury progression and rescue-throughput rules; they do not show that expanding capacity or fleets always improves outcomes.
