# Santa Lucia flood-response model

This SVN working copy is the authoritative implementation for the
Santa Lucia study. It adapts the simulation code underlying
[Flood-ABM (Addai et al., 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijdrr.2025.105698);
it is not the original Flood-ABM release or a claim to have developed
that framework. The study contributes the Santa Lucia adaptation,
scenario experiments and their analysis. The upstream repository is
[uofs-simlab/flood_abm](https://github.com/uofs-simlab/flood_abm).
The original local checkout and public repository agree on reference commit
`41cb39e30d76e9986b1b1e7d977910efcae8975a` (11 August 2025); this is not
proof that the adaptation began at that revision. Its MIT copyright and
permission notice is retained in [UPSTREAM-LICENSE.txt](UPSTREAM-LICENSE.txt).
The author confirmed that this repository supplied the adapted code;
the verified reference identifies the audited upstream source, while
the original copying date is not recorded. Licence selection for
study-specific additions and spatial-data redistribution permissions
remain separate release decisions.

The sibling
`flood_columbia_paper` working copy contains the manuscript and generated
publication material, not another simulation implementation. SVN records
source history; do not create parallel model-version directories.

## Model assumptions

- **Population:** 972 synthetic people, 286 mapped homes, four businesses,
  three schools, and shelter, healthcare and government facilities.
  Individual age, wealth, education and identity proxies are generated by
  [person_agent_assign.py](agents/person_agent_assign.py) using
  [colombia_calibration.py](agents/colombia_calibration.py).
- **Households:** balanced synthetic sizes, with an adult aged 18 or older
  assigned to every home before the remaining people are shuffled.
  The standard population produces 172 three-person and 114 four-person
  households. Each person has exactly one home. Individual marginal
  attributes are preserved, but household wealth homogeneity, family
  relationships and the household-size distribution are not empirically
  calibrated. A population with insufficient adults or people for the
  mapped homes is rejected explicitly.
- **Hazard:** the supplied flood geometries define active exposure
  footprints. Depth remains a stochastic draw at each query, modified by
  the organization attenuation parameter. These are scenario assumptions,
  not a validated hydraulic reconstruction.
- **Rescue:** fractional throughput carries forward between hours; unused
  whole slots expire. Shelter places and the two-hour minimum stranding
  duration also constrain admission. Admission events and distinct rescued
  people are counted separately.
- **Protection:** sheltered and hospitalized people do not independently
  evacuate or undertake exposed daily movement. Their facility membership
  is exclusive. A sheltered person can return only after 12 elapsed hours
  and only when their home does not intersect any active flood footprint.
  A return explicitly moves the person home. The return decision does not
  use a single stochastic building-depth draw as evidence that the flood
  has ended.
- **Healthcare:** eligible injured people transfer out of shelter when a
  bed is available. After all hourly actions, every living person who
  remains injured accrues exactly one injury hour, regardless of location,
  admission or flood phase. The same injury-survival deadline applies in
  and out of care; continuous stranding retains its separate exposure
  deadline. Hospital care recovers people whose fixed recovery propensity
  is at most 0.9 and resets injury time. Evacuees have assumed off-site
  access to the same recovery rule, without occupying local beds. These
  clinical and external-access assumptions are not empirically calibrated.
  The evacuation flag records geographic absence independently of survival;
  off-site deaths still enter population mortality.
  Death removes facility membership. A recovered patient returns home if safe, otherwise transfers to
  shelter if a place is available. With neither destination available,
  the recovered person remains protected in hospital and continues to
  occupy a bed. This conservative discharge assumption needs sensitivity
  analysis, not a claim of operational calibration.
- **Capacity:** nominal percentage capacities retain their integer-admission
  interpretation: 1%, 10% and 30% of 972 allow 10, 98 and 292 shelter
  occupants. Healthcare at 5% permits 49 patients.

The organization parameter has separate physical-attenuation and scalar
social-capital channels. Rescue throughput depends on route distance,
water/road speeds, fleet size and trip capacity. There are no explicit
boats, queues or travel trajectories.

## ODD+D description and literature context

The existing [manuscript](../flood_columbia_paper/flood_columbia.tex)
contains an ODD+D appendix grounded in the executed source: entity/state
inventory, randomized hourly schedule, design concepts, initialization
and parameter distributions, external inputs, movement and clinical
submodels, all 17 theory-selection entries, stochastic action tests and
an implementation crosswalk. A scheduling diagram and six documentation
tables supplement the unchanged numerical results.

The description distinguishes evacuation's exposure consequences from
preparedness/coping/adaptation flags. Preparedness is reset before
during-flood decisions and coping before post-flood decisions; neither
these flags nor planned adaptation directly changes physical protection.
SCT means social capital, not social cognitive theory. There is no
explicit social network, fitted learning process or collective household
decision model.

The manuscript now contains 21 references, including ODD/ODD+D,
protective decisions, flood precaution, social capital, shelter
location/allocation, disaster logistics, sensitivity and validation.
These sources motivate and contextualize mechanisms, not validate
transferred coefficients or mixed-year local input proxies.
The documentation-only revision preserves all 480 primary and 960
sensitivity runs and all 45 generated publication assets; no simulation
rerun or model-source change is involved.

## Targeted sensitivity study

The follow-up design in [design.py](run/sensitivity/design.py) uses the
same standard implementation and reuses the matching 480-run primary cohort. It varies one
assumption at a time around seven primary settings:

- warning lead time: 1 and 3 days versus 7;
- hospital capacity: 1% and 10% versus 5%;
- people per boat trip: 3 and 9 versus 6;
- depth attenuation: 0.25 and 0.75 versus 0.5;
- social-capital boost: 0.15 and 0.45 versus 0.3.

Inactive combinations are omitted: attenuation changes only the
full-organization setting, and social boost changes full and social-only
organization. There are **48 new settings x 20 common seeds = 960 new
runs**. These ranges are assumption stress tests, not empirical
uncertainty distributions. The design is fixed after primary analysis
and before sensitivity execution; it does not claim global or joint-factor
sensitivity.

```powershell

# This freezes the sensitivity design; it does not run simulations:
& $py -B run\sensitivity\design.py
.\run\hpc\richardson.ps1 -Study Sensitivity -Action Deploy
.\run\hpc\richardson.ps1 -Study Sensitivity -Action Gate
# After all four primary reference repetitions match exactly:
.\run\hpc\richardson.ps1 -Study Sensitivity -Action Run -Workers 64
.\run\hpc\richardson.ps1 -Study Sensitivity -Action Status
# Optional bounded wait, followed by verified import, analysis and PDF build:
.\run\hpc\richardson.ps1 -Study Sensitivity -Action Await
# After remote completion:
.\run\hpc\richardson.ps1 -Study Sensitivity -Action Pull
```

The sensitivity cohort uses [data_collection/sensitivity/](data_collection/sensitivity/)
and the existing Richardson source directory, with distinct transfer and

refuses changes to existing frozen core source or inputs.

records are not relabeled or overwritten.
`Await` waits up to six hours; the local workstation and SSH connection
must remain available for automatic retrieval and manuscript generation.
Remote `tmux` jobs remain independent of that waiting connection.

[sensitivity_results.py](analysis/sensitivity_results.py) reuses the full
independent hourly-output verifier. It estimates four seed-paired
mortality contrasts and their changes from primary references.
Changes are paired differences of differences, not differences of
independent intervals. Exact sign-flip tests retain integer death counts;
Holm correction covers all 30 applicable modifications. Pointwise 95%
Monte Carlo intervals quantify finite-seed precision, not structural or
empirical uncertainty.

